# Projecting 2027 state-level armed conflict (baseline scenario)

A multi-step projection to 2027. **This is a scenario projection, not a validated forecast**: no
2027 data exist, and 2026 is itself only 10 months observed. Uncertainty is therefore large and is
propagated explicitly. Outputs: `outputs/projection_2027.csv`, `outputs/map10_projection2027.png`,
`outputs/fig_national_fan_2027.png`.

## Method
Using the same pooled persistence + spatial-spillover model (β₁ = 0.75 own-lag, β₂ = 0.04 neighbour-lag;
fitted on 2015–2025 transitions), 2027 was generated by **Monte-Carlo recursive simulation** (5,000
draws): the 2026 state vector was anchored on the real Jan–Oct 2026 data (annualised ×12/10) with
added uncertainty for the two unobserved months, the model was applied one step to 2027, and an
**empirical residual was resampled** for each draw. The resulting distribution compounds (a) 2026
input uncertainty and (b) the 2027 process shock. Point summaries are the simulated median and mean;
intervals are simulation percentiles.

## Results
**National.** Projected 2027 total: **mean ≈ 15,500, median ≈ 13,500 deaths**, 80% interval
**[9,400, 22,300]**. In plain terms, the baseline is that national violence stays near its current
elevated level (~13,000–16,000), with a wide band that spans a meaningful decline to a further
escalation (see the fan chart). The mean exceeds the median because the distribution is right-skewed.

The **North-West/North-Central belt remains the projected epicentre**, and the diffusion-front states
(**Kaduna, Kebbi**) stay elevated relative to their 2025 base.

## Why the central path dips (read before quoting the numbers)
The projected **medians fall below 2026** — e.g., Borno's median (1,860) is far under its 2026
annualised (4,951). This is **statistical mean-reversion**, not a substantive prediction of peace: an
autoregressive model with persistence < 1 pulls extreme values toward the long-run mean over multiple
steps. The realistic reading is the **upper half of the interval** for currently-escalating states;
Borno's 80% band reaches 6,179, covering continuation at present intensity. For national totals the
**mean (~15,500)** is the better central estimate than the median.

## Hard limits (essential caveats)
- **No validation is possible** for 2027; treat as a conditional baseline ("if current dynamics
  persist"), not a prediction.
- **Compounding error + mean-reversion** make multi-step central paths conservative and biased toward
  the mean; intervals are wide by construction (σ(log) ≈ 1.03 per step).
- **Structural breaks are invisible** — new armed actors, major operations, or a political shock
  (note: Nigeria's next general elections fall in early 2027, a plausible violence driver the model
  cannot encode).
- 2026 inputs are preliminary ACLED data subject to revision.

## Bottom line
Under a continuation-of-current-dynamics baseline, 2027 conflict is projected to remain at broadly
2025–2026 levels (national ~13,000–16,000 deaths, wide band), concentrated in the North-West and
North-Central, with the diffusion front (Kaduna, Kebbi) persistent. The 2027 election cycle is a
salient upside risk the model cannot quantify.

Source: ACLED via HDX (data to 30 Oct 2026); boundaries OCHA/HDX; population NBS/NPC.
